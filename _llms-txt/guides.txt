# Deep links, permissions and a clean slate

> Open a deep link, grant or revoke a permission, force-stop the app or wipe its data from the App row.

**Why:** a push notification should open the product page, the app should survive a user who refused the camera, and onboarding should run again after a reset. Each of these is one click here, no adb commands needed. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) Open the App row on the App tab of the Device panel: it holds the app’s actions. 1. **Deep link.** A deep link is an address that opens a specific screen inside the app, like `myapp://checkout?id=42` or an `https://` link the app claims. Type it into the Deep link field and press Open. **Only in this app** sends it to your app directly; without it Android picks the app, as a tap would. 2. **Permissions.** Click a permission to grant it, click again to revoke it. 3. **Force stop** kills the app; **Restart app** starts it cold. 4. **Clear data** wipes everything the app stored: it’s back to the welcome screen, as after a fresh install. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Links you opened are remembered in your browser, so a repeated check is one click. * Clear data can’t be undone. If you want to come back to the current state, press **Save app data** first (see [Keep sign-in between sessions](/guides/app/app-data/)). * **Restart app** and **Save app data** work without ADB; deep links, permissions, force stop and clear data need the device’s ADB, which the page opens in the background. That’s why the row may say “Connecting to ADB” for a moment. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The deep link opens nothing**: the app doesn’t handle that address. Check the scheme and the path with the developers, or try without **Only in this app** to see whether any app takes it. * **The buttons are greyed out**: ADB isn’t available to you, for example in someone else’s session. Only the session’s owner and admins get these tools by default. More details: [ADB tools](/concepts/adb/).

# Keep sign-in between sessions

> Save the app's data when you stop a session and start the next one already signed in, past onboarding.

**Why:** you test the checkout every day, and every day you sign in, confirm a code and skip five onboarding screens first. Save that once and start from it. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) **App data** is what the app keeps on the device for itself: who is signed in, settings, databases, the WebView’s cookies and local storage. 1. Get the app into the state you want: signed in, onboarding done, a test cart filled. 2. Press Save app data in the App row, or simply Stop the session: stopping saves the data too. 3. Next time, in the launch dialog, pick your save instead of **Fresh install**. Tick Also restore the cache if you want cached files back as well. 4. The progress list shows Restoring app data, and the app opens where you left it. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Saves are **yours only**: nobody else sees them, admins included. The **App data** page in the dock lists them; you can download or delete them there. * Only a few recent saves per app are kept, and saves nobody used for a while are deleted (by default 6 saves and 7 days). * The quick launch button (the lightning icon) can start straight from your newest save. * A save from a **newer build** may not work in an older one, for example when its database can’t be downgraded. The launch dialog warns you. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no App data section in the launch dialog**: saving is turned off on this server. Ask whoever runs Holtdroid. * **The app signs you out anyway**: some apps encrypt their data with keys that stay on the original device (Android Keystore). Such data can’t move to another device; this is a limit of Android, not of the save. * **Stop takes long**: it waits for the save to finish. A big app with a big cache saves longer. More details: [App data](/concepts/app-data/).

# The app crashed: find out why

> Go from the crash notification to the exact error in Logcat, filter it and download it for the bug report.

**Why:** “the app closes when I tap Buy” is a bug report developers can’t do much with. The stack trace from the device tells them the file and the line. Here you get it in two clicks. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) **Logcat** is Android’s own log: apps and the system write into it what they do, and a crash leaves its full error there. 1. The app crashes: Android shows “keeps stopping” on the screen. 2. Holtdroid notices it and shows a notification with the first lines of the error. Press Logcat in it. 3. The Logcat tab opens. Red lines are errors: the exception name, its message and the lines starting with `at` show where in the code it happened. 4. Narrow it down: This app / Everything switches between your app’s lines and the whole device, the level filter keeps only errors, and the text field finds a tag or a word. 5. Download saves what is shown, ready to attach to the ticket. 6. The app stays closed after a crash: a card over the screen says so, with **Reopen**. Restart app in the App row brings it back too. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * The log is collected from the moment the device is ready, and survives app restarts and installing another build. You can find a crash that happened ten minutes ago. * **This app** follows your app by its user id, so it covers all of its processes and keeps working after restarts. * For a bug report with everything at once (screenshot, log, device details), use the **Bundle** button in the Device row. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **No notification appeared**: the app may have closed without a crash, for example it called `finish()` or was killed for memory. Look at Logcat with **Everything** and the **Error** level. * **The Logcat tab is missing or says it’s waiting**: the tab works over the device’s ADB, which isn’t available to you in someone else’s session. * **The error is in the web part of the app** (a white screen, a broken button): look in the [DevTools console](/guides/web/devtools/) instead; JavaScript errors don’t crash Android apps. More details: [Logcat](/session-tools/logcat/), [ADB tools](/concepts/adb/).

# Files on the device

> Look into the app's private folder, download its settings and databases, and put test files on the device for file pickers.

**Why:** to test an “attach a document” form you need a PDF on the phone, and to check what the app really saved after onboarding you need its settings file. On a normal phone neither is easy. Here it’s the Files tab. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open the Files tab next to DevTools. 2. **App data** is the app’s private folder, `/data/data/<package>`: * `shared_prefs` holds the app’s settings as small XML files; * `databases` holds its SQLite databases; * `app_webview` holds the WebView’s cookies, local storage and IndexedDB. Then download a file to open it on your computer. 3. **Shared storage** is what other apps see: Download, Pictures, DCIM. That’s where a file picker looks. 4. Open Download and press Upload, or drop files from your computer onto the list. The file appears on the device, ready to pick in the app. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * You can also create folders and delete files, for example to check how the app reacts when its database is gone. These two buttons show up only where the provider lets ADB run any command (`redroid.shell: full` on emulators, `adb.shell: full` on phones). * **/data/local/tmp** is a scratch folder anyone can write to; handy for test files that shouldn’t show up in the gallery. * Everything you change disappears with the device when the session stops. To keep the app’s own data, use [Save app data](/guides/app/app-data/). ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The app doesn’t see the uploaded file**: on recent Android versions apps see shared storage only through the system file picker or after the user allowed access. Pick the file from the app’s own “choose file” button. * **The tab is missing or waiting for ADB**: Files works over the device’s ADB, which isn’t available to you in someone else’s session. More details: [Files](/session-tools/files/), [ADB tools](/concepts/adb/).

# Install another build in the same session

> Switch the running device to another build of the app, with or without its data, without launching a new device.

**Why:** a bug shows up in the release candidate, and you want to know whether production had it too. Or you need to check that an update from the store version doesn’t lose the user’s cart. Both are one switch away. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Click the app’s name in the session header. **Switch version** opens. 2. It lists every build with the same application ID that the device runs, from every source, with its branch, uploader and age. Search by version, branch or uploader. 3. Decide about Keep app data: * **on** (the default): the build is installed over the current one, upgrade or downgrade. The app keeps its sign-in and data, so you see how its storage migrates; * **off**: the current build and its data are removed first, as on a fresh device. 4. Click the build you need. The same device installs it, and the header shows the new version. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * The device, its settings and your other changes stay: country, network, language, the clipboard. Only the app changes. * Going **down** with data kept tests a downgrade, which many apps don’t support: their database may refuse to open. The list marks such builds. * To compare two builds side by side, launch a second session instead. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **“It is signed with a different key”**: Android won’t install a build over one signed by someone else (for example a debug build over a release one). Turn off **Keep app data** and install it cleanly. * **The install failed**: the previous build keeps running, nothing is lost. The message says why. * **The build isn’t in the list**: it has another application ID (a `.debug` suffix, say), so it’s a different app for Android. Launch it from the Apps page. More details: [Sessions and slots](/concepts/sessions/#the-app-inside-a-session).

# Clipboard between computer and phone

> Copy on your computer, paste on the device, and the other way round, so test data and links never have to be typed by hand.

**Why:** a 30-character promo code, a test card from the wiki, a link the app generated for sharing. Typing them on a phone is slow and error-prone; copying is instant. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The Clipboard row of the Device panel shows whether sync is on and in which direction. A click on the row turns it on or off. 2. Open it with its chevron to choose the direction: * Both ways: copy on either side, paste on the other; * To the device: only what you copy on the computer goes to the phone; * From the device: only what the app copies comes to your computer. 3. To paste, tap a field on the phone and press Ctrl+V or Cmd+V. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Ctrl+V or Cmd+V on the screen **always** pastes, even with sync off. Sync is needed for the other direction and for the app’s own Paste button. * The panel keeps a short history of what went back and forth in this browser tab, with buttons to copy an entry again or save it as a text file. * Pasted text goes into the device’s clipboard quietly, without Android’s clipboard preview popping up. * Only the session’s owner gets clipboard sync: someone watching a shared session doesn’t receive your clipboard. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **Nothing reaches the device on its own**: the browser doesn’t let the page read your clipboard. Allow clipboard access for the portal in the browser, or just press Ctrl+V or Cmd+V on the screen. * **There is no Clipboard row**: you are watching someone else’s session, or this device doesn’t support it. * **The app’s copy doesn’t arrive**: check that the direction is Both ways or From the device. More details: [Sessions and slots](/concepts/sessions/#the-app-inside-a-session).

# Language, time, theme and battery

> Switch the device to a dark theme, another language or time zone, and a nearly empty battery, without digging through Android settings.

**Why:** half of UI bugs show up only for some users: dark theme, long German words, another time zone, a phone at 5%. Each of them is one click away in the rows of the Device panel. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Display: turn on Dark theme and look for text that disappears and hard-coded colours. Brightness and font size are here too. 2. Language: pick a system language. Android switches without a reboot; check translations, long words and date formats. 3. Time: change the time zone, and on a real phone move the clock itself to test expiring offers and subscriptions. 4. Battery: set a low charge, charging or battery saver. 5. Every changed row is marked Changed, so you see what differs from a normal phone. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * These settings apply to the whole device, not only to your app, and stay until the session ends or you change them back. * A virtual device runs on the server’s clock: its time zone can change, the time itself can’t. Real phones can move the clock or set an exact moment; turning **Real time** off stops Android from syncing it back. * The Language row also adds keyboard layouts, so the on-screen keyboard can type in that language. Some real phones can’t change the system language without root; there the row sets the app’s own language instead. * Battery saver unplugs the charger, because Android turns the saver off while charging. On some real phones the charge level can’t be faked, only the saver. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The app ignores the dark theme**: it doesn’t follow the system theme. That is worth a bug report on its own. * **The language didn’t change in the app**: the app may have its own language setting, or read the language only at start. Restart the app. * **There is no way to shift the clock**: you are on a virtual device; only the time zone can change there. More details: [Device controls](/configuration/emulator/#device-controls).

# See where the app's traffic goes

> Read every DNS lookup, point a name at a staging server, and find far servers and wasted connections in the routes.

**Why:** a slow screen is often not the app but the network around it: a CDN on another continent, a name that doesn’t exist, a new TLS connection for every request. And to test against staging you shouldn’t need a special build. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The DNS tab lists every name the device looks up, as it happens. 2. Marks show what stands out: NXDOMAIN for a name that doesn’t exist, far when every address is on another continent than the VPN exit, and DoH blocked for encrypted DNS that would skip the log. 3. A row’s details show the `CNAME` chain and each address with its country and network. 4. Rewrite this name answers it with your own address, blocks it or makes it not exist. 5. The Routes tab follows the connections from the device through the VPN to each server, with no reuse, far, bypass and HTTP flags. 6. Flagged leaves only the names that look off. ## Examples [Section titled “Examples”](#examples) * **Production app against staging.** Rewrite `api.example.com` to the staging server’s IP address and restart the app. Turn the rule off to go back; with TTL 0 it applies at once. * **What if analytics is down?** Rewrite the analytics domain to **Doesn’t exist** or **Block** and check that the app still opens and doesn’t hang on a spinner. * **Why are images slow in Kazakhstan?** Switch the [country](/guides/conditions/vpn/) to Kazakhstan and look for **far** in Routes: an image CDN without an edge nearby shows up right away. * **Too many handshakes.** **no reuse** on your API means every request opens a new TCP and TLS connection. Send the connection list from the details to the backend team. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Both tabs need a session with a VPN location: its sidecar sees the device’s DNS and connections. * Rewrites clear Android’s DNS cache, but a WebView can keep a name for up to a minute. Restart the app when a rewrite must apply to everything. * Save a set of rewrites under **Sets** and load it into another session later. * [Recordings](/guides/recordings/record-share/) keep both tabs, so a link to a recording shows the DNS answers and connections at the moment of the bug. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no DNS or Routes tab**: the session has no VPN location. Physical phones have Routes but no DNS tab yet. * **A rewrite has no effect**: the app hard-codes an IP address or resolves names over its own DNS over HTTPS. * **Countries are missing**: this Holtdroid has no geolocation databases configured. More details: [DNS log and rewrites](/session-tools/dns/), [Routes](/session-tools/routes/).

# GPS location and routes

> Put the device anywhere on the map or send it along a route, to test maps, delivery and anything that asks for the location.

**Why:** the delivery screen should pick the nearest store and show the courier moving. You can’t walk to Almaty to check it, but the device can. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The Location row of the Device panel shows where the device is. By default it follows the VPN country. 2. Open it to see the map. Click anywhere or drag the dot to move the device. 3. Or paste exact coordinates and press Enter. 4. The app gets the new location from Android, the same way it gets a real GPS fix: here the delivery address changes. 5. Route moves the device along a path at walking or driving speed. 6. **Reset** in the panel brings the location back to the VPN country. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Once you set the location by hand, Follow the VPN turns off. Turn it on again to keep GPS and the IP address in the same country. * The app must have the location permission. If it asks, allow it on the device, or grant it in the App row (see [Deep links, permissions and resetting the app](/guides/app/app-actions/)). * Changing the GPS doesn’t change the IP address: for a full “user in another country” set the [VPN location](/guides/conditions/vpn/) too. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The app doesn’t react**: it may cache the last location. Restart the app, or check that it has the location permission. * **The map is empty**: the map tiles are turned off on this Holtdroid. Coordinates still work. * **The dot jumps back**: Follow the VPN is on and the VPN location changed. Set the place by hand again. More details: [Device controls](/configuration/emulator/#device-controls).

# Slow or unstable internet

> Put the device on 3G, a weak signal or a subway ride and see how the app copes with delays, losses and drops.

**Why:** in the office everything is fast, but users open the app on the train. Check that it shows loaders, retries and clear errors instead of a blank screen or a double payment. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open the Network row of the Device panel. Its chips switch the common networks; its **Network conditions** row opens the network picker with every network the session can emulate. 2. On the **Networks** tab, pick a named network such as 3G. Each one shows its delay, bandwidth and loss and a short description. 3. Use the app and watch how it behaves: slow images, late prices, timeouts. 4. Scenarios like Subway, Driving or Elevator change the network over time, with short periods of no signal. The **Scenarios** tab draws their steps and marks the one running now. 5. Airplane mode, Wi-Fi and Mobile data turn the network off completely. 6. Pick No emulation in the picker’s header to get the normal connection back. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Only the apps’ traffic is slowed down. The screen, DevTools and ADB stay fast, so you can still work comfortably. * During an outage in a scenario Android still thinks it is online: requests hang until they time out, like on a dead cell. Airplane mode is different: Android knows the network is gone and apps are told so. * Regions add the typical round trip of users in a country to the CDN and to your servers. Combine them with a [VPN location](/guides/conditions/vpn/) of the same country. * The numbers are realistic averages from public reports, not measurements of a specific operator. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no Network conditions row**: emulated networks need VPN locations; this Holtdroid has none configured. * **The app is faster than the chosen network promises**: the device’s VPN route is already slower than that network. The picker warns about it; pick a closer VPN location. * **Everything is slow, including the screen**: that is your own connection, not the emulation. More details: [Network](/session-tools/network/), [Network conditions](/configuration/emulator/#network-conditions).

# Switch the country (VPN)

> Make the device look like it is in another country, on the fly, to test regional prices, content and restrictions.

**Why:** a user from Kazakhstan says checkout shows the wrong price, and you are in the Netherlands. Switch the device to Kazakhstan and see exactly what they see. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The Network row of the Device panel shows the current country and the device’s public IP. 2. Open it with its chevron and pick another country under VPN location. The switch takes about a second; the app keeps running. 3. Refresh the screen in the app and check what changed, for example the prices. 4. To see what the server answered, open the request in DevTools. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * **All** of the device’s traffic goes through the chosen country: every app, and DNS lookups too, so nothing gives the real location away. * The country is also picked in the launch dialog; the one you used last time is remembered. * The VPN changes the IP address only. GPS, language and the time zone are separate: set them in their own rows if the app checks them (see [GPS location](/guides/conditions/gps/) and [Language, time, theme and battery](/guides/conditions/device-settings/)). * Sessions on the same country share its IP address. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no VPN location in the panel**: this Holtdroid has no VPN configured. Ask whoever runs it. * **The app still shows the old country**: many apps detect the region once and remember it. Restart the app, or launch with a fresh install. * **The switch spins and fails**: that location is unreachable right now. Pick another one or try again later. More details: [Per-session VPN](/concepts/vpn/).

# Compare the screen with the design mockup

> Lay a Figma mockup over the phone screen, let Holtdroid find every difference and send the designer a report.

**Why:** “the button looks a bit off” is hard to argue about. Laying the mockup over the real screen shows exactly what is off and by how many pixels, and the report settles it. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open the Pixel perfect tab next to DevTools. 2. Add the mockup: drop an image, choose a file or paste it with Cmd+V (Ctrl+V). It becomes a reference and lies over the screen. 3. Use Opacity to see more of the app or more of the design. 4. Press Find differences. Each one gets a plain explanation: shifted, another colour, missing. 5. Click a difference to find it on the screen. 6. Export report saves a picture to attach to the task. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * From Figma, SVG is the best format: select the frame and **Copy as → Copy as SVG**, then paste it into the tab. It stays sharp at any size. Keep the frame the size of the phone screen in dp (for example 360 × 800). If a frame can’t be SVG, export PNG at 3×. * References belong to you, not to the session: they are there in every session, the current app’s first. * The phone keeps working under the mockup: taps go through it. * Something on the screen is gone in a second (a toast, an animation)? **Freeze** keeps the current picture so you can compare it calmly. * A blend called **Difference** turns matching pixels black and lights up what is off: a quick check by eye. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The mockup is blurry or the wrong size**: it was probably a PNG at 1×. Use SVG, or PNG at 3×. * **Everything is a difference**: the mockup isn’t lined up. Turn on **Move**, drag it into place, then compare again. * **A video or map always differs**: hide that part of the mockup or skip that area of the screen in the tab, so it isn’t compared. More details: [Pixel perfect](/session-tools/pixel-perfect/).

# Connect your computer

> Run one command from the Computers page and the devices you launch show up in adb and Android Studio on your own machine.

**Why:** you want to debug the app from Android Studio, run `adb` commands or open `chrome://inspect` on your laptop, while the device itself runs in the portal. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open Computers (the laptop button in the dock at the bottom) and press **Get the command**. 2. Copy the command and paste it into a terminal on your Mac or Linux machine, or in WSL on Windows. 3. The script downloads the `holtdroid` client, checks it and connects. Your computer appears on the page as online. 4. If `adb` is installed, every device you run is already connected: `adb devices` lists it. 
```sh
curl -fsSL https://holtdroid.example.com/tunnel.sh | sh -s -- eyJhbGciOi…
```
 ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * ADB (Android Debug Bridge) is the tool that talks to an Android device from your computer: install apps, read logs, open a shell. Android Studio uses it too. * Each device gets its own port on your computer, from 15555 up, for example `127.0.0.1:15555`. The Computers page and the device’s SSH row show which one is which. * The client keeps running in the terminal and reconnects by itself when the network drops or the laptop wakes up. Press `q` to quit. * The code in the command works once and only for 10 minutes. Next time run `~/.cache/holtdroid/holtdroid`, or install the `holtdroid` command from the client’s **Settings** and just run `holtdroid`. * You don’t need SSH keys or an `ssh` command: the client makes its own key and never touches `~/.ssh`. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The Computers button is missing**: the SSH gateway is turned off on this portal. Ask whoever runs it. * **The command says the code expired**: get a fresh command on the Computers page. * **The device isn’t in `adb devices`**: check that `adb` is on your `PATH`; otherwise connect by hand with `adb connect 127.0.0.1:<port>`, taking the port from the Computers page. * **Windows**: works through [WSL](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/wsl/install), with a few limits. Run the command in a WSL terminal. If `adb` and Android Studio are installed on Windows rather than in WSL, connect them by hand with `adb connect 127.0.0.1:<port>`: WSL shares its localhost ports with Windows. **Enter** and **w** in the client may not open the Windows browser; open the portal and `chrome://inspect` there yourself. More details: [Connecting your computer](/concepts/ssh/).

# Open your local dev server on the phone

> Publish a port from your computer, and the app or browser on the device reaches it as localhost, with hot reload.

**Why:** you’re building the web part of the app on your laptop and want to see it inside the real app’s WebView on a real Android, without deploying anything. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. [Connect your computer](/guides/developers/connect-computer/) first and leave the `holtdroid` client running. 2. Start your dev server as usual, for example Vite on port 5173. 3. The port shows up on the Computers page. Click it to publish it on your devices; in the default **Dev servers** mode the client publishes servers like `node`, `bun` or `vite` by itself. 4. On the session page, the SSH row (App tab of the Device panel) lists the published ports. 5. On the phone, open `http://localhost:5173`: the page comes from your laptop. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * A port is the “door number” a program listens on: your dev server listens on 5173, so it is `localhost:5173`. Publishing makes the same door open on the device. * A published port goes to every device you run, including the ones you start later, and only to yours. * Plain `http://localhost` works even if the app forbids unencrypted traffic: Android makes an exception for localhost. Other hosts still follow the app’s rules. * Hot reload works: save a file and the page on the phone updates. * In **Everything** mode every listening program on your computer is published, desktop apps included. The client asks before switching to it. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The port isn’t on the Computers page**: make sure the client is running and the server listens on `localhost`, not only on another address. * **Red port in the SSH row**: nothing answers on that port on your computer. Start the server, or check the number. * **The page loads but API calls fail**: the app still calls the API by its normal address. Point it at `localhost` too, or publish the API’s port as well. * **A port is skipped on emulators**: a few ports are taken by the session itself (5555, 5038, 27042, 18580, 18581, 7890, 9090). Use another one. More details: [Connecting your computer](/concepts/ssh/).

# Launch an app on a device

> Pick a build, choose the Android version, screen and country, and get a working phone in your browser in under a minute.

**Why:** to try a build without installing it on your own phone, on exactly the Android version and screen you need, and to throw the device away when you’re done. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. On the Apps page every app is a row with its newest build. Press Launch. The arrow at the end of the row opens its other builds, each with its own launch button. 2. At the top of the dialog, choose where it runs: a **virtual device** (Android in a container, any version) or a **physical phone** connected over USB; each shows how many are free. The preview on the right draws the phone you’re putting together and sums up your choices. 3. Set up the device: Android version, screen, WebView, country and app data. The defaults are sensible: if you don’t know what to pick, leave them. 4. Press Launch and wait for Ready. It usually takes less than a minute. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Every launch is a **clean device**: nothing from the previous session is left on it, unless you start from saved [app data](/guides/app/app-data/). * The device is yours alone until you press **Stop** or leave it idle; then it goes back to the pool. * The VPN location, network, language and other settings can be changed **during** the session, so you don’t need to relaunch to try another country. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **Launch says “Join the queue”**: all devices are busy. Stay on the page: the device starts as soon as one frees up. * **An Android version is greyed out**: the app doesn’t support it (its `minSdk` is higher). Hover it to see why. * **A provider or phone is greyed out with “Doesn’t run this build”**: the build has native code only for another processor (an x86 build on an ARM phone, say) and there’s no build of this version for the device’s. * **The device failed to start**: the reason is shown on the progress card. Launch again; if it repeats, send the message to whoever runs Holtdroid. More details: [Sessions and slots](/concepts/sessions/).

# Time limits and how many devices you get

> When a device is taken back, how to keep it with Extend, and what the device pool and per-person limits mean.

**Why:** devices are shared. Knowing when one is taken back saves you from losing a half-reproduced bug when you come back from lunch. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The Devices card on the Apps page lists every running device, yours first, and its bar shows the pool, one tick per device: green ones run, blue ones are starting, amber ones are about to stop, grey ones are free. 2. When a device gets close to being stopped, its badge turns amber and shows how long is left. Two clocks run at once: * **idle time**: a device nobody uses is stopped after 30 minutes by default; * **maximum lifetime**: every device is stopped after 8 hours by default, used or not. 3. Extend (next to the badge, and in the **⋯** menu of every row) restarts the idle clock. It’s also in the session header. 4. When you’re done, press Stop: the device goes back to the pool right away. ## What counts as using the device [Section titled “What counts as using the device”](#what-counts-as-using-the-device) * clicks, typing, scrolling or touches anywhere on the session page, while the tab is visible; * changing a setting, switching the location, restarting the app, pressing a phone key, using the clipboard; * pressing **Extend**. A tab that is just open in the background does **not** keep the device alive. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Your server may use other values than 30 minutes and 8 hours; the countdowns always show the real ones. * Your server may limit how many devices one person runs at once. Your limit is in **Account and passkeys** in the user menu. * When the pool is full, a launch waits in line and starts as soon as a device frees up, as long as you stay on its page. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **“You already run as many devices as you may; stop one first”**: you hit your limit. Stop a device you no longer need. * **The device disappeared while you were away**: it was idle too long. Next time press Extend before you leave, and [save the app data](/guides/app/app-data/) if signing in again is tedious. * **Extend and Stop are greyed out** (the **⋯** menu of a row): it’s someone else’s session; only its owner and admins can manage it. More details: [Activity and reaping](/concepts/sessions/#activity-and-reaping).

# Launch in one click

> The lightning button starts a device right away with your last settings, fresh or from your saved app data.

**Why:** when you test the same app every day, the launch dialog asks the same questions every time. The lightning button skips it. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Next to **Launch** in every app row, and next to every build when you open a row, is the lightning button. It launches with the provider, VPN location and other choices you used last time, or the defaults if you never launched. 2. Hover it to see the device it will use and, when your server saves app data, how the app should start: - Fresh install: a clean app, nothing saved; - With app data: your newest save of the app, so you’re already signed in; - With app data and cache: the same, with the cache too. 3. Pick one and the device starts straight away. A plain click on the button later repeats your last pick. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * The choice is remembered in your browser, so another browser or computer starts with the defaults. * Without app data on your server the button has no menu: a click just launches. * When all devices are busy, a quick launch waits in line like a normal one. * Saves come from earlier sessions; see [keep your sign-in between sessions](/guides/app/app-data/). ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **With app data starts a fresh app**: you have no save of this app yet. Run a session and save its data first. * **It launched on the wrong Android version or country**: it repeats your last launch. Use **Launch** once with the settings you want; the lightning button follows from then on. More details: [App data](/concepts/app-data/).

# Find your way around a session

> What every part of the session page is for, from the header to the Device panel, the screen with its toolbar and the tools on the right.

**Why:** the session page packs a lot into one screen. Two minutes here and you’ll know where to look for each thing instead of hunting for it mid-test. ## What’s where [Section titled “What’s where”](#whats-where) 1. **The header** is about the session: the app and its build (click it to install another build) with the device, Android, WebView and country under it, the state badge, the time left, Extend and Stop. 2. **The Device panel** on the left holds the device’s controls in three tabs: Environment, Media and App. A row’s chevron opens it in place, like the Network row: change the country, the network, the GPS position, the battery, the language or the clock. 3. **The screen** in the middle is the real device. The toolbar beside it has Back, Home, Recent apps, rotation, a screenshot, volume, power and Restart app. 4. **The tools on the right** are tabs: DevTools, Logcat, DNS, Routes, Files and Terminal, with WebSockets, Cache and Pixel perfect under **More**. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * A row marked **Changed** differs from the default, and its tab gets a dot, so you can see at a glance what you changed. * Right-click the screen to wake the device when its display is off. * If the app closes or crashes, it stays closed; **Reopen** on the card over the screen starts it again. * Some rows and tabs only appear when your server turns the feature on, so your page may have fewer of them. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The screen stays on “Connecting to the screen…”**: reload the page. If it keeps happening, try a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. * **Extend and Stop are greyed out**: the session belongs to someone else. You can watch it, but only its owner and admins can manage it. More details: [Sessions and slots](/concepts/sessions/).

# Upload your own APK

> Put a build from your computer into Holtdroid, decide who sees it and how long it stays, and launch it right away.

**Why:** not every build comes from CI or Slack. A debug build from your laptop or an APK someone sent you can be tested the same way, without installing it on your phone. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Press Upload APK on the Apps page. 2. Drop the .apk or click to choose it. Dropping it straight onto the list of apps works too. 3. Decide who can see it: everyone, or only you (admins see private builds as well). 4. Leave Temporary on for a throwaway build, or untick it to keep the build. 5. Write a note and, if you like, a changelog, so others know what to test. 6. Press Upload. The build shows up in its app’s row, and the launch dialog opens right away. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Holtdroid reads the app’s name, package, version and icon from the APK itself, so a new build of a known app lands in that app’s row. * Temporary builds are deleted once nobody has launched them for a while: 7 days by default, your server may use another value. Each launch restarts that clock. * You can change the visibility, make a build permanent or delete it later from its menu in the app’s list of builds. * The APK is installed exactly as uploaded; Holtdroid never changes it. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no Upload APK button**: uploads are turned off on your server. Ask whoever runs it. * **“The file is larger than the upload limit”**: the server caps the size (1 GiB by default). Ask for a higher limit or use a smaller build. * **The row says “Reading package info…”**: the APK is being parsed. If it stays like that, the file may be broken or not an APK. More details: [APK sources: uploads](/sources/overview/#uploads).

# A bug report in a minute

> One click in the Device row bundles a screenshot, the logs and the device details into a zip for the ticket.

**Why:** a ticket that says “the button doesn’t work” comes back with “which phone, which Android, which build, any logs?”. A bundle answers all of that before anyone asks. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open the Device row on the App tab of the Device panel. It shows the model, the Android version and the build. 2. Press Bundle. A few seconds later a zip is downloaded: a screenshot, the logcat collected so far, and the device and app details. 3. Attach the zip to the ticket. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Logcat is the phone’s system log: what the app and Android wrote while you were testing. Crashes and errors land there, which is why developers always ask for it. * **Full report** next to Bundle runs Android’s own `bugreport`. It is much bigger and slower; use it only when a developer asks for it. * To keep the evidence after you stop, or to show it to someone without an account, [record the session](/guides/recordings/record-share/) and share the recording. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **Bundle is greyed out or the row says ADB is unavailable**: the browser couldn’t connect to the device’s ADB (the Android debugging channel). Wait a few seconds after the device starts; on some portals ADB is turned off. More details: [ADB tools](/concepts/adb/).

# Record a session and share it

> Pin markers while you test, watch the recording with its logs and network, and send a link that opens the exact moment.

**Why:** a bug showed up once and you’re not sure you can repeat it. With a recording, the developer sees the screen, the logs and every request of that very run, instead of reading “it sometimes fails at checkout”. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. While a session is recorded, the Recording row of the Device panel (Media tab) shows the elapsed time. 2. When something goes wrong, click the row (or the clock in the header) or press Mark this moment: a marker is pinned on the recording’s timeline. 3. Open Recordings in the dock and pick the recording. It plays like a video, with logcat, the WebView’s network and console moving along with the picture. 4. Click a marker to jump to it, and look at the Network tab for the failed request. 5. Press Share: a public link to this moment is copied. Paste it into the ticket. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Depending on how your portal is set up, sessions are recorded **always**, **unless you turn it off** in the launch dialog, or **only when you turn it on**. If the row says “Not recording”, click it to start. * A recording keeps more than the picture: logcat (the device’s system log), the WebViews’ requests, console and WebSockets, the device, network and load, and your taps. Typed text is never recorded. * A share link opens the recording **without signing in** and carries the moment you’re at, so the reader lands right on the bug. Shared recordings and recordings with markers are not deleted automatically. * Very short recordings (under 20 seconds by default) are thrown away when they end. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **No Recording row or no Recordings button**: recordings are turned off on this portal. Ask whoever runs it. * **The link button fails**: share links are turned off on the portal; download the recording file instead (**Export → Recording file**). * **The recording ends early**: it ends when the session stops. Stopping and starting it again makes a new recording. More details: [Recordings](/concepts/recordings/).

# Inspect the web inside an app with DevTools

> See the requests, console errors and HTML of the web pages inside an app, with Chrome's own developer tools, right next to the phone.

**Why:** many apps show part of their screens as web pages. When something breaks there, DevTools shows which request failed and which error the page threw, so the bug report says what went wrong, not just “it doesn’t work”. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. The DevTools tab sits next to the phone. These are Chrome’s developer tools, connected to the app’s **WebView**: the built-in browser engine that shows web pages inside apps. 2. Network lists every request the page makes. A red line failed; click it to read the headers and the response. 3. Console shows the page’s messages and errors with the file and line they came from. 4. Elements shows the HTML and styles, for layout bugs. 5. Keep using the phone: everything new appears as it happens. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * Holtdroid turns WebView debugging on when the app starts. The APK is not changed or rebuilt. * The bar above DevTools shows the page’s address. Type another address and press Enter to open it in the WebView; the button on the left reloads the page. * When the app has several web pages or processes, pick one in the target list next to the address. * An iframe (a page inside a page) is not a separate target: open Console and choose it in the `top ▾` menu at the console’s top left. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **DevTools stays empty, “Waiting for a WebView…”**: the screen the app shows right now has no web content. Open the part of the app that does. * **On a real phone DevTools is empty**: on a phone without root only debuggable (development) builds expose their WebViews. Ask for a debug build or use a virtual device. * **The app closes right after the start**: some apps refuse to run on rooted or emulated devices. Try a real phone. More details: [WebView DevTools](/concepts/devtools/).

# Follow live updates in the WebSockets tab

> Read the messages a web page and its server push to each other, and find the one that broke the screen.

**Why:** chats, live prices and order statuses arrive over WebSockets, not ordinary requests, so the Network list doesn’t show them. This tab does, and shows the exact message that came before a bug. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Open the WebSockets tab. A **WebSocket** is a connection that stays open, so the server can send updates whenever it wants. 2. Each line is one message: an arrow up was sent by the app, an arrow down came from the server, with its length and time. 3. Click a message, such as the price update, to read it in full. 4. Look for the message right before things went wrong: here the order failed with `out_of_stock`, and the app never told the user. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * The tab works without opening DevTools. * Holtdroid keeps the session’s socket traffic from the moment the app started, so the tab shows it all even after you reload the portal page or open the session on another computer. * **Clear** empties the list in your browser only. **Hide under 15 B** leaves out pings and other tiny messages. Pause stops the list; resuming fills in what came meanwhile. * Event streams (another way servers push updates) show up here too. * While the session is recorded, click a message’s time to put a marker on the recording at that moment. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **The list is empty**: the page hasn’t opened a socket yet. Open the part of the app with live data. * **A socket is marked as video and has no messages**: video streams are left out on purpose, they would flood the list. More details: [WebSockets](/session-tools/websockets/), [WebView DevTools](/concepts/devtools/).

# Test on another WebView version

> Start a device with an older, newer or built-in WebView to catch bugs that only one browser engine version has.

**Why:** a web page inside an app can break on one WebView version and work on the next. When a user reports a bug you can’t reproduce, the WebView version is often the difference. ## What happens [Section titled “What happens”](#what-happens) 1. Press Launch, keep Virtual device selected at the top. 2. Pick the Android version first: the WebView list then shows only builds that run on it. 3. Choose a WebView. **WebView** is the browser engine that draws web pages inside apps; every app on the device uses the same one. The newest stable Chromium is preselected; Built-in is the one that came with the Android image; Beta shows what users get soon. 4. Launch. A chosen Chromium build is installed before the app starts. 5. During the session, the Device row (App tab of the Device panel) can switch the WebView provider. Restart the app afterwards. ## Good to know [Section titled “Good to know”](#good-to-know) * The session header shows the installed WebView version, so it ends up in screenshots and bug reports. * To compare two versions, start one session for each and keep them side by side. * The Chromium builds are close to what users get from the Play Store, but not identical: fixes that landed in the release branch late are missing. The page engine itself (Blink and V8) is the same. * Chromium builds can’t play H.264 or AAC video. If your bug is about video, use Built-in or a Google WebView card when your Holtdroid offers one. * Your choice is remembered and reused by quick launch. ## If it doesn’t work [Section titled “If it doesn’t work”](#if-it-doesnt-work) * **There is no WebView section**: it needs a WebView catalog on the server. Real phones get it only when the catalog has Google WebView builds for ARM: their Android takes no other WebView. * **The version you need isn’t listed**: it may not run on the chosen Android version (for example, Chromium 149 and later need Android 14 or 15). Pick a newer Android. * **Video doesn’t play in the page**: see the codec note above. More details: [WebView versions](/concepts/webview-versions/).
