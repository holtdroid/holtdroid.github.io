Session tools

Images

The images the app's WebViews load, checked against how their pages draw them, with what each one could save.

The Images tab lists what is wrong with the images the app’s WebViews load while you use it: images that come in but never show up, files many times bigger than the place that draws them, PNGs and JPEGs that would be a fraction of the size as WebP, and WebPs saved at a quality close to 100. Each finding says how many bytes it would save. It is for QA reporting a slow first screen and for developers deciding which images to fix first.

It needs no DevTools window: the session’s agent follows every page and iframe of the WebViews from their first request (see DevTools), so the tab shows images that loaded before you opened it, also after a reload of the session page.

When the tab appears

Section titled “When the tab appears”

The tab sits next to DevTools in every session whose device supports DevTools. How much it can tell depends on the image scan:

  • Off (the default): only the files themselves are checked, their format and compression. A note at the top says This session doesn’t check where its pages draw images, with a Turn on button.
  • On: the agent also runs a small script in the app’s pages (each page and iframe) once a second that reports which images they draw, where and how big. Only then can the tab tell images that are on screen from those that aren’t, and compare a file’s size with the size it is drawn at.

Switch on Check how pages draw their images in the launch dialog to have the scan from the start; the dialog remembers the choice in this browser. Turn on in the tab starts it in a running session: it sees what the pages show from then on, so reload the page in the app to also check how it loads. Image scan on · turn off in the toolbar stops it and takes the script out of the pages. The setting belongs to the session, so it changes the SVG tab and everyone watching the session too.

The APK isn’t touched, but while the scan runs the pages have a window.__holtdroidImages object and two observers (a MutationObserver and an IntersectionObserver).

What you see

Section titled “What you see”

The toolbar has Pause and Clear on the left and the number of images on the right. Pause stops following the WebViews; on resume the tab catches up with what the agent kept meanwhile. Clear empties the tab.

The numbers on top

Section titled “The numbers on top”
  • Images: every image file the WebViews loaded, one per URL however often it was requested.
  • Downloaded: what they took over the network, cache hits excluded.
  • Not on screen (only with the image scan): the bytes of images that weren’t shown within 10 s of loading, or that nothing drew.
  • Could save (only when there is something to save): what would be left out if every image were as small as it can be: not loaded at all, at the size it is drawn, or as WebP.

Findings

Section titled “Findings”

Below, the findings, warnings first and then by how much they save. The chips above them filter by severity: All, Warnings (amber triangle) and Hints (grey circle). Each row has the finding, the bytes it could save (hover for whether that is measured or estimated) and ×N, the number of requests for the file.

Finding Severity When
… wasn’t on screen in the 10 s after it loaded warning The page draws it, but below the fold, outside the viewport, or only in elements that are hidden. The saving is the whole file: it could be loaded later, with loading="lazy".
… loaded but the page draws it nowhere warning No <img>, CSS background, SVG <image> or poster used it. Maybe a preload for a later screen, maybe not needed.
… loaded 12.3 s before it was shown hint It did show up, but more than 10 s after it arrived.
… is 1024×1024 but drawn at 84×84 device pixels at most warning at 4× the pixels or more, else hint The file is at least 1.5 times bigger than needed in both directions and at least 10,000 pixels go unused.
… is PNG: as WebP it would be about 41 KB instead of 75 KB warning when it saves 20 KB or half the file, else hint PNG, JPEG, GIF and BMP, when WebP saves at least 1 KB and 10%.
… looks barely compressed: re-encoded it is … warning A WebP or AVIF that shrinks by at least 2 KB and 30% when re-encoded as WebP at quality 85.
… is heavy for its size: 3.1 bits per pixel hint A WebP over 2.5 or an AVIF over 1.5 bits per pixel, 8 KB or bigger, when the file couldn’t be re-encoded to measure it.

The on-screen checks need the image scan and skip favicon.ico, images only workers loaded, and images that loaded before the pages were first scanned (they get no not on screen or late findings). “Drawn at” counts the screen’s density, object-fit and background-size; an image used in several places is judged by the largest. SVG and ICO files only get the on-screen checks.

Measured or estimated. For the last 300 images (SVGs aside) the tab asks the agent for the file the WebView received and your browser decodes it and re-encodes it as WebP at quality 85, whole and at the size it is drawn. When the WebView no longer has the file, or it is over 1 MB, the saving is a guess from what WebP usually saves (a PNG to about 55%, a JPEG to 70%, a GIF to 60%, a BMP to 15%), and the finding says about. GIFs are always a guess, since re-encoding would lose an animation’s frames.

A finding’s details

Section titled “A finding’s details”

Click a finding to open it below the list (click it again, or the cross, to close it). Its explanation comes first, then:

  • Image: a preview on a checkerboard, the format, the file size, the natural size, drawn at most in device pixels, and when it was measured WebP q85 (the whole image as WebP) and WebP at the drawn size. The full URL is underneath.
  • Drawn by (with the image scan): each element that used it, with the device’s time of its first use, what it is (<img>, CSS background, SVG <image>, video poster or image button), its box in CSS pixels times the screen’s density, its object-fit or background-size, and whether it was on screen, off screen (amber) or hidden. On screen means inside the phone’s screen: an image in an iframe that is scrolled away or clipped counts as off screen.
  • Requests: every request for the file with its time and size, or the cache it came from (memory, disk, service-worker, prefetch).

Common tasks

Section titled “Common tasks”

Find what slows the first screen. Launch with Check how pages draw their images, open the screen, wait ten seconds and look at the warnings. Wasn’t on screen and draws it nowhere are bytes the first screen paid for and didn’t show; Not on screen adds them up.

Check a fix. Clear the tab, reload the page in the app and compare Downloaded and Could save with before.

Report an image. Open the finding: the URL, natural size, drawn size and the WebP sizes are what a developer needs to export it right. With a recording, Open in Network takes you to the request itself.

Look at an image that doesn’t show. Its Drawn by rows say whether the element was off screen (the page could load it lazily) or hidden (it was downloaded for nothing).

In recordings

Section titled “In recordings”

A recording has the same tab with the same checks, judged up to the playhead: a time there seeks the video and Open in Network opens the request. Recordings keep image files up to 512 KB to measure; without the image scan they get only the checks on the files, and say so.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • Images inlined as data: or blob: URLs, redirects and failed requests (4xx and 5xx) aren’t listed.
  • The tab follows the last 5000 requests and 3000 scan reports, and the agent keeps 8 MB of requests and scan reports for a page that reconnects; in a very long session the oldest drop out.
  • The scan reports at most 300 image uses per frame each second and looks at CSS backgrounds again at most every 3 seconds, after the page changed. Pages in the background report nothing.
  • Files are measured from the WebView’s own copy, which it keeps only while it has room (32 MB in all, 2 MB a file). A file it already dropped is judged by a guess, marked so.
  • A page’s own srcset choice is what is judged: the device’s screen density decides which candidate it loads.
  • Savings are byte counts of the files. They don’t count decoding time or memory, which also shrink with smaller images.
