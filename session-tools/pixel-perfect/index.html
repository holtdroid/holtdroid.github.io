Pixel perfect

Lay a design mockup over the device screen, find where the app differs from it and export a report.

The Pixel perfect tab next to DevTools lays a design mockup over the device’s screen, the way the PerfectPixel browser extension does for web pages, and goes a step further: it finds where the screen differs from the mockup, explains each difference and makes a report to send to whoever owns it.

References

Section titled “References”

A reference is an image: SVG, PNG, JPEG or WebP, up to pixelPerfect.maxFileSize (25 MiB). Add one by dropping it on the tab, choosing a file, or pasting it with ⌘V / Ctrl+V while the tab is open.

From Figma, paste the link

For a Figma frame, paste its link instead of an exported image (see Import from Figma): it comes with its layers and components, rendered by Figma itself. Keep the frame the size of the phone’s screen in dp (for example 360 × 800); Holtdroid fits it to the work area. An exported image still works: a PNG at 3× or 4×, or an SVG for a flat frame (Figma’s SVG export loses corner smoothing and background blur).

An SVG needs a size: width and height on its root, or a viewBox, which Figma always writes.

Import from Figma

Section titled “Import from Figma”

A frame can come straight from Figma, together with its layers: select it, Copy link to selection (⌘L / Ctrl+L), and paste the link into the tab, or use Figma in the reference list. Figma renders it as a PNG at 4× (less for very tall frames, to stay under 24 megapixels): its own rendering, exact for corner smoothing, shadows, blurs and text, and the same as the components it is split into. There is no format to choose.

Components

Section titled “Components”

The import also renders up to 20 parts of the frame on their own, so you can leave some out of the reference, such as a placeholder where the app shows a video, a camera picture or a map. The parts are the frame’s children; while there is room, the largest group or frame that adds nothing of its own (no fill, stroke, effect, clipping, mask or opacity) is replaced by its children, so a wrapper is split and a card stays whole. What is meant as one thing stays one part:

  • a component instance (a button, a timer, a card), unless it covers at least half of the frame;
  • anything smaller than 5% of the frame;
  • a list or grid: a group whose items, looking through plain rows, are mostly (three quarters or more) instances of one component or layers of one name.

A frame with more than 20 children keeps them as they are, up to 60. References imported before these rules get them on Update from Figma.

The Components tab lists them, top first, with a picture each; pointing at one outlines it on the screen and the eye hides it. Hidden components are kept with the reference. With all of them shown, the overlay is the frame’s own rendering; with some hidden, the browser draws the frame again from its background colour and the visible components, in order. Skip hidden components (on by default) also leaves their place out of the comparison, so the video under a hidden placeholder isn’t a difference.

Parts with an image or video fill over at least half of them (a photo or a player, not an icon), or whose name says video, player, stream, camera, preview or map, are marked live?, and Hide live content hides them at once.

Redrawing works when the frame’s own fill is plain colour and no mask spans its parts; otherwise the tab says so and ignored areas are the way to skip them. A redrawn frame can differ from Figma’s rendering where hidden and shown parts interact: background blur over a hidden part, or blend modes between them. The pictures count towards your quota (usually a few MB) and are fetched again by Update from Figma, which keeps the hidden ones that still exist.

Importing needs a Figma personal access token. The server may have one for everyone (pixelPerfect.figma.token); otherwise add yours on the Pixel perfect page (the frame icon in the dock), under Account in the users sign-in mode, or right in the import form. Each of them has the same step-by-step guide:

  1. In Figma, click your name or avatar in the top left corner → Settings → Security.
  2. Under Personal access tokens, Generate new token, name it holtdroid and pick the longest expiration Figma offers.
  3. Scopes: File content read-only and Current user read (Holtdroid checks the token with it and shows whose it is); nothing else.
  4. Copy the token (it starts with figd_, Figma shows it once), paste it and Check and save.

The token reads only the files your Figma account can open. Where it is kept depends on the server:

  • With your account when the database is sqlite and auth.sessionKey is set: the api checks it with Figma, encrypts it (AES-GCM with a key derived from auth.sessionKey) and never sends it back, so it works in every browser you sign in with. Changing auth.sessionKey makes saved tokens unreadable; everyone then saves theirs again.
  • In this browser otherwise (an in-memory database would lose it on the next restart): the api only checks it, and the browser sends it with each import or update.

When the token expires, imports say Figma refused it: generate a new one and Replace it.

Pixel perfect page

Section titled “Pixel perfect page”

The frame icon in the dock opens the page for your references outside a session: all of them grouped by app, with search, the space they take and when they will be deleted; import from Figma or add an image, rename, download, delete, open a Figma reference’s frame in Figma or update it, and show or hide its components (the layers button). It also holds your Figma token and how the tab starts in this browser (opacity, blend, and which of the switches below are on).

The import keeps the layers under the frame: their boxes, the top fill and stroke, corner radius, opacity, font (family, weight, size, line height, letter spacing) and auto layout padding and gap. They move with the reference when you move or scale it, and the comparison uses them (see Elements). Update from Figma (the arrows next to a reference) fetches the frame again, keeping where it lies and its ignored areas.

A phone frame drawn at the screen’s width in dp (360, 393, 412) has its measures in dp; a wider frame is in pixels, and the tab says so. References belong to you, not to a session, so they are there in every session; the ones added for the current app come first.

Double-click a name to rename it. Each reference also remembers where you put it over the screen and which areas the comparison skips.

They live in the server’s storage (S3 or a folder). Each user has pixelPerfect.quota (1 GiB by default; per user in the users auth mode), and a reference nobody opened for pixelPerfect.maxAge (30 days) is deleted; laying it over a screen counts as opening it, its thumbnail in the list doesn’t.

The list shows small previews (160 px wide, the top of tall frames) that the browser makes when you add an image, so opening the tab doesn’t download the full images; the server makes them for PNG, JPEG and WebP it got without one.

The tab

Section titled “The tab”

The toolbar at the top holds what is used all the time: the reference (click it for the list, search, import from Figma or add an image), the eye that hides the overlay, its opacity and blend, Freeze, the settings and Find differences. Below it, three tabs:

  • Layout: the work area, the position, the grid and the guides;
  • Components (or Skip areas for an image): the Figma components to hide and the ignored areas;
  • Differences: the result, which Find differences switches to.

When the panel is wide (DevTools stretched across the window), the three are shown side by side as columns instead of tabs.

The same panel is in a recording’s player, over the recorded video (see Recordings).

Nothing of Pixel perfect is drawn over the screen while another tool tab (DevTools, Logcat, …) is open: the reference, a frozen frame, the differences, the work area, the grid and the guides come back as they were when you return to the tab.

Over the screen

Section titled “Over the screen”

Picking a reference lays it over the device screen. By default it fills the work area’s width without going past its height, top-aligned, so a mockup at any density (1×, 2×, 3×) fits as is.

  • Opacity and the blend: Normal is the mockup see-through; Difference is the classic “negative” check, where matching pixels turn black and anything off lights up; Invert shows the inverted mockup, so matching areas fade to grey.
  • Position: X and Y in the mockup’s own pixels and a scale, where 100% is the fit above. Turn on Move to drag it over the screen or nudge it with the arrow keys (Shift for 10 px). While dragging, its edges and centre snap to the work area, the grid’s columns and the guides; hold Alt not to. The device stays usable while Move is off: taps go through the overlay.
  • The eye button hides the overlay without losing its settings.

Work area

Section titled “Work area”

The work area is the part of the screen you work in, for example the app without the status and navigation bars, or a single card. The reference is fitted to it, the grid and the guides start at its corner, and the comparison skips everything outside it. The area outside is dimmed and the area outlined in amber.

  • Whole screen, the default.
  • Insets: top, right, bottom and left in dp, so the same area fits any screen size and density. Without system bars sets 24 and 48 dp.
  • Drawn: drag a rectangle over the screen; it is kept as fractions of the screen.

Detect takes a screenshot and reads the views, and offers what it finds as insets: Content by colour cuts off plain bars along the edges (black bars above and below a letterboxed game; a camera hole or a few status icons in them don’t stop it), a large WebView, SurfaceView, TextureView or player view, and the App window. Point at one to see it outlined on the screen, click to use it. When the content reaches the screen’s edges and fills it, there is nothing to find.

While drawing the work area, an ignored area or a guide, a loupe next to the pointer shows the screen magnified with its pixel grid. Corners land on pixel boundaries and snap to the nearest contrasting edge within four pixels, such as a card’s border; hold Alt to place them freely. The loupe uses a picture of the screen taken when the tool is turned on, or the frozen frame.

Each reference keeps its own area, grid and guides. Save as a preset (the bookmark button) keeps them under a name for your other references and devices, and the list above the switch applies one; presets live with your account in the server’s storage.

Grid and guides

Section titled “Grid and guides”

The grid is drawn inside the work area, in dp: a number of columns with a margin on each side and a gutter between them, like Material’s layout grids, and rows every so many dp for a baseline. 4 columns, 8 columns and 8 dp rows fill in common values.

Guides are lines at a distance in dp from the work area’s left or top edge. Turn on Rulers and drag from the ruler at the top of the screen for a horizontal guide, or from the left one for a vertical guide; drag a guide to move it (it snaps to the area and the columns) and drag it out of the area to remove it. They are also listed with their positions to type in exactly.

Grid and guides on the screen in the settings hides them without losing them.

Freeze frame

Section titled “Freeze frame”

Freeze frame keeps the current picture of the screen over the live one, so a toast, a ripple or an animation that is gone in a second can still be compared: freeze it while it is on the screen, then line the mockup up and look for differences at leisure. Live (on the screen or in the tab) goes back to the stream.

Finding differences

Section titled “Finding differences”

Find differences compares the screen with the mockup as it lies:

  1. The screen is taken from a lossless screenshot of the device, or from the frozen frame. A frozen frame comes from the stream, so a higher stream quality makes it sharper.
  2. Pixels are compared perceptually (pixelmatch, YIQ colour distance), with anti-aliased edges left out. Transparent parts of the mockup are not compared, and lone stray pixels (video compression, dithering) don’t make a region.
  3. Differing pixels are grouped into regions, and each region gets a guess at what is wrong: shifted by so many pixels in which direction (the mockup is moved around the region to find where it fits), colour (same shapes, another colour, with both colours), missing on the screen, not in the reference, or simply differs. Sizes are in the device’s pixels.

The match is the share of compared pixels that agree. Regions are outlined and numbered on the screen (Differences on the screen in the settings turns that off) and on the Differences tab, which offers the views that image diffs on code review sites use: Side by side, Swipe (a line to drag across), Onion skin and Difference (black where equal, the size of the error otherwise, with the differing pixels in red). Pointing at a region in the list highlights it everywhere; a click opens a magnified crop of the reference, the difference and the screen.

While a frame is frozen, moving or scaling the mockup or changing the ignored areas runs the comparison again at once, which helps line a mockup up.

Interfaces over video

Section titled “Interfaces over video”

An interface drawn over a video, a camera picture or a map would differ from any mockup everywhere the video is. Three things keep that out:

  • Skip what moves (on by default) takes a few frames a moment apart before comparing, marks the parts of the screen that changed between them and leaves them out: video and animation under the interface are skipped, the still interface over them is compared. The skipped area is shaded blue. Freeze frame takes these frames right after the frozen one.
  • Ignored areas: on the Components tab (Skip areas for an image without components), turn on Draw and drag over the screen to mark areas the comparison always skips for this reference, such as a player that may be paused.
  • Skip video, canvas and frames in WebViews (with Name differences by WebView elements on, see below): in a WebView page, the boxes of <video>, <canvas>, <iframe>, <embed> and <object> elements are left out, paused or not (see below). They are hatched violet.

Elements

Section titled “Elements”

A difference found by pixels says where; elements say what is there. Both switches are in the settings (the sliders icon in the toolbar).

Name differences by native views (on by default) reads the app’s views with Android’s uiautomator, the way Appium does, without touching the app: each difference names the view that fits it, with its class, resource id, text or content description, and its size in pixels and dp; pointing at a difference outlines the view over the screen. Compose screens show their texts and descriptions, and their test tags as ids when the app sets testTagsAsResourceId. It adds a second or two, and doesn’t work while the screen keeps animating: the tab then says so and compares by pixels alone. With a frozen frame, the views are read the moment it is frozen.

With a reference imported from Figma, each difference also names the design layer there (◆ Card › Pay, outlined in pink when you point at it) and what the design asks of it: size, fill, stroke, radius, font, padding and gap; for a colour difference, the fill next to the colour on the screen. The view or page element drawn where that layer is (outlined in amber) is measured against it, so the list says width 320 dp instead of 328 and the magnified crop has a table of the edges and sizes that are off.

WebView pages

Section titled “WebView pages”

This part is experimental and off by default: turn on Name differences by WebView elements in the settings; the choice is remembered in the browser.

When the screen shows a WebView that DevTools can inspect, the comparison also asks the page about itself, through its own DevTools connection next to the DevTools tab (Chrome serves several at once):

  1. It reads the visible elements of each open page: their boxes, a CSS selector, their own text and a few computed styles, and takes a screenshot of the page.
  2. It finds where that screenshot sits on the device’s screen, which places the page on it exactly: under the status bar and the app’s own toolbar, or wherever the app put its WebView. A WebView that isn’t on the screen, or a page in a background WebView, doesn’t match and is left out. The page found is outlined violet on the screen.
  3. Each difference gets the element that fits it best: the list shows button.submit “Pay” under the difference, and the magnified crop adds its selector (with a copy button), its size in CSS pixels, a shift in CSS pixels as well as device pixels, and the styles that matter for that kind of difference: margins, padding and transforms for a shift, colours and borders for a colour difference, sizes and fonts otherwise.
  4. Pointing at a difference in the list highlights its element on the device the way DevTools does, with margins, padding and its info box.

With a frozen frame, the page is read the moment the frame is frozen, so a toast that is gone by the time you compare still names its element. Highlighting needs the element to still be there.

Report

Section titled “Report”

Report downloads a PNG with the project’s logo, the reference name, the app, its version, the screen and the time, the match, three columns (the reference, the difference with ignored areas, the screen) with the regions numbered on them, and the list of differences with their sizes, positions, their elements or views, and the Figma layer with what is off.

Configuration

Section titled “Configuration”

Without a storage of its own the references go to storage, which the Helm chart puts on the cache volume, an emptyDir: give them an S3 prefix or a persistent folder to keep them across restarts.
