Session tools

Network

Slow, lossy or dropping networks for the whole device, and a region's round trips to CDNs and servers, on top of the session's own route.

Apps are built on fast office Wi-Fi and used on a train. The Network tab puts the device on a slower network: a named link such as 3G or weak 4G, a scenario that changes the link over time (a subway ride, an elevator), and optionally a region, the round trips that users in a country have to a CDN and to your servers. Use it to see whether the app shows loaders, retries and clear errors instead of a blank screen.

When the tab is there

Section titled “When the tab is there”

The tab appears in emulator sessions with a VPN: the conditions are applied to the VPN tunnel, so without VPN locations configured there is no tab. Phones don’t have it (see Phones). The Network card beside the phone shows the current conditions, and its Network conditions row (Slow links, scenarios and regions in the Network tab) opens the tab.

What it affects

Section titled “What it affects”

The conditions apply to the whole device: every app, the WebView, native code, DNS lookups. They are not DevTools’ throttling, which slows down one page only. What Holtdroid itself uses to reach the device (the screen, DevTools, ADB, the agent) isn’t slowed down, and neither are the addresses in vpn.bypassCIDRs.

An outage in a scenario drops every packet while Android still sees a connected network, so requests hang until they time out, as on a dead cell. For a network that Android knows is gone, turn on Airplane mode, or turn Wi-Fi and Mobile data off, in the Network card.

The conditions stay when you switch the VPN location; the tab then measures the new route (see The session’s route).

What you see

Section titled “What you see”

The heading says what is on: No emulation, the network’s name (3G), or the region with its link (Germany · typical mobile). No emulation next to it turns everything off.

The session’s route

Section titled “The session’s route”

The box at the top, The session’s own route, through its VPN location, shows what the session’s network already takes before any emulation:

  • CDN edge 23 ms: the round trip to Cloudflare’s nearest edge;
  • ↓ 48 Mbit/s: the route’s download bandwidth;
  • the round trip to each server location (Frankfurt 31 ms · N. Virginia 112 ms · …);
  • how old a measurement is, when it is over two minutes (12 min ago).

Every emulated network comes on top of it: a network adds its delay to these round trips, and can’t be faster than the route’s bandwidth. Round trips are measured every minute; the bandwidth after a location change, or when the last test is over six hours old. A new session starts from the last route measured through its location. Until the first measurement the box says Measuring…. How the agent measures is described under Network conditions.

Access link

Section titled “Access link”

One card per network. Each shows its name, a short description and its numbers: the delay it adds with its jitter, the download and upload bandwidth, the packet loss (+70 ±20 ms · ↓ 10 Mbit/s ↑ 3 Mbit/s · 0.5% loss). The picked card is outlined. Without a region, a card also shows the round trip it means to a CDN edge (≈ 93 ms to a CDN edge), the route’s plus its own delay.

Network Delay Download Upload Loss

Loss is per direction. “In bursts” means lost packets come in runs, as on a weak cell or a busy hotspot, rather than one at a time. The numbers are rough medians from public mobile network reports, rounded to be easy to remember, not measurements of a specific operator. There are no custom values: pick the closest network.

Amber notes on a card say where the route spoils it:

  • The VPN route’s N ms is more than this network’s own N ms: the route is already slower than the network would be (5G behind a far VPN location), so the delay ends up larger than named;
  • Capped at about N Mbit/s by the VPN: the network is faster than the route’s bandwidth.

The card can still be picked: loss, jitter and slower links are unaffected.

Region

Section titled “Region”

Region stands in for the app’s users in a country. Pick one of Germany, the United States, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Bangladesh, India, Pakistan, Indonesia, the Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt or Brazil (or No region), then where your servers run in Servers in: Frankfurt (the default), N. Virginia, N. California, São Paulo, Mumbai, Singapore or Tokyo. Each server location in the menu shows the region’s round trip to it, and the route’s in amber when the route is slower (160 ms · VPN 210 ms). A region the route is slower to is marked VPN slower: with CDN, servers or bandwidth.

A region splits the device’s traffic into two paths. The table below the menus shows them:

Column What it is
Path CDN edge and DNS (the address ranges of Cloudflare, Fastly and Google, served from the edge the region’s users reach, and every DNS query), or Servers in … (your backends and everything not on a CDN)
In the region the round trip the region’s users have on that path, beyond their access link
Session route what the session’s route already takes on it; amber when that is more than the region’s
Added what the tab adds: the region’s round trip less the route’s, never below 0

The access link comes on top of both paths. With No limits picked as the access link, the first card becomes Typical mobile in the region, the region’s median mobile connection, and that link is used.

When the route is already slower than the region on a path, a warning says so: emulation can only add delay, so a VPN location closer to the servers, or none, gets nearer.

Backends behind a CDN

Section titled “Backends behind a CDN”

An API behind Cloudflare has a CDN address, but its requests go through to your servers. List such hosts under Backends behind a CDN, one per line (or separated by commas): a host name, *.example.com for every name below it, or an IPv4 address. Save backends appears when the list changed.

Their traffic then takes the servers’ path. The agent answers the device’s DNS, so it picks up the addresses the device gets when it looks a backend up, and shapes them about a second later; the tab lists the addresses found so far. Other hosts on the same CDN addresses take the servers’ path too.

The list is kept per app: in your account when the portal’s database is persistent, in this browser otherwise. It is filled in when you pick a region in the app’s next session.

Common tasks

Section titled “Common tasks”

Check the app on a slow network. Pick 3G or Weak 4G and go through the main screens: loaders should show, and nothing should be tapped twice while a request is slow.

Check that the app survives drops. Pick Subway, Elevator or Flapping. Requests made during an outage hang until they time out; the app should retry or say what happened. The WebSockets tab shows each reconnect of a socket as a new row.

Test for users in a country. Pick the country as the region, your servers’ location in Servers in, and list your API under Backends behind a CDN. A VPN location in or near the same country keeps the route short, so the tab can add the region’s full round trips; the IP address then also matches the country.

Find out why the emulated network is faster than promised. Read the amber notes: the route is already slower than the network, or the network faster than the route’s bandwidth. Pick a closer VPN location.

See how long requests took. The tab only sets the conditions; the timings are elsewhere (see Measuring the effect).

Measuring the effect

Section titled “Measuring the effect”

Holtdroid has no separate load-speed report. Request timings are in these places:

  • live, in the DevTools tab’s Network panel: Chrome’s own timing for each request of the WebView;
  • in a recording, a request’s Timing on the Network tab: queued and stalled, DNS, connect (with TLS), send, Waiting (TTFB), download and the total;
  • in a recording’s Routes tab, the median connect, TLS and waiting times of the WebView’s requests to each name, and how many of them opened a new connection;
  • the Network card’s Device traffic: download and upload of the whole device every two seconds over the last minute.

In recordings

Section titled “In recordings”

A recording keeps the conditions as they changed: the player’s Network card shows the VPN location, Throttling with the network’s name and numbers (or Full speed), the Region with what it added to the CDN and the servers (CDN +5 ms · Frankfurt +40 ms) and the device’s traffic, each as it was at the moment you are at.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • Only emulator sessions with VPN locations have the tab; phones don’t.
  • Emulation can only add delay and take bandwidth away, never make the session’s route faster.
  • Addresses are IPv4 only; the CDN ranges are Cloudflare’s, Fastly’s and Google’s published lists.
  • There are no custom networks: the cards and scenarios are fixed.
