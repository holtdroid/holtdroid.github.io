Network

Slow, lossy or dropping networks for the whole device, and a region's round trips to CDNs and servers, on top of the session's own route.

Цей контент ще не доступний вашою мовою.

Apps are built on fast office Wi-Fi and used on a train. Network conditions put the device on a slower network: a named link such as 3G or weak 4G, a scenario that changes the link over time (a subway ride, an elevator), and optionally a region, the round trips that users in a country have to a CDN and to your servers. Use it to see whether the app shows loaders, retries and clear errors instead of a blank screen.

Where to find it

Section titled “Where to find it”

Network conditions are there in emulator sessions with a VPN: the conditions are applied to the VPN tunnel, so without VPN locations configured there are none. Phones don’t have them (see Phones).

The Network row of the Device panel (tab Environment) shows the current conditions and, opened, everything for a quick change:

  • Conditions: chips for No limits, Typical 4G, 3G, 2G (EDGE) and Subway (plus the current network when it is another one), and All…, which opens the picker;
  • Ping, Mbit/s and Loss of what is applied right now: the network’s own numbers, or the current step’s while a scenario runs (Subway · step 2 of 3: 3G);
  • VPN exit, the VPN location with the external address;
  • Wi-Fi, Mobile data and Airplane mode, and the device’s traffic;
  • Network conditions, a row with the current summary that opens the picker too.

The row is marked Changed while anything is emulated or a radio is off; Reset in it turns everything back.

What it affects

Section titled “What it affects”

The conditions apply to the whole device: every app, the WebView, native code, DNS lookups. They are not DevTools’ throttling, which slows down one page only. What Holtdroid itself uses to reach the device (the screen, DevTools, ADB, the agent) isn’t slowed down, and neither are the addresses in vpn.bypassCIDRs.

An outage in a scenario drops every packet while Android still sees a connected network, so requests hang until they time out, as on a dead cell. For a network that Android knows is gone, turn on Airplane mode, or turn Wi-Fi and Mobile data off, in the Network row.

The conditions stay when you switch the VPN location; the agent then measures the new route (see The session’s route).

The picker

Section titled “The picker”

The picker opens over the session. Its header says what is on: a running scenario and its step (Subway · step 2 of 3), or the network’s name, or the region with its link (🇩🇪 Germany · typical mobile). No emulation next to it turns everything off; the cross or Esc closes the picker, and every pick applies at once.

Below the header are three tabs, Networks, Scenarios and Region, each with how many it offers, and on the right the Session route: the CDN edge round trip, the nearest server location and the route’s bandwidth (CDN 18 ms · Frankfurt 34 ms · ↓ 94 Mbit/s).

The session’s route

Section titled “The session’s route”

The session’s own route (in the Region tab while no region is picked, and in short next to the tabs) shows what the session’s network already takes before any emulation:

  • CDN edge 23 ms: the round trip to Cloudflare’s nearest edge;
  • ↓ 48 Mbit/s: the route’s download bandwidth;
  • the round trip to each server location (Frankfurt 31 ms · N. Virginia 112 ms · …);
  • how old a measurement is, when it is over two minutes (12 min ago).

Every emulated network comes on top of it: a network adds its delay to these round trips, and can’t be faster than the route’s bandwidth. Round trips are measured every minute; the bandwidth after a location change, or when the last test is over six hours old. A new session starts from the last route measured through its location. Until the first measurement it says Measuring…. How the agent measures is described under Network conditions.

Networks

Section titled “Networks”

The Networks tab is the access link: one row per network with its name, a short description, the delay it adds with its jitter (RTT), the download and upload in kbit/s, the packet loss and a Speed meter (green, amber or red, from GPRS to 5G on a log scale, less for delay and loss). The picked row has its radio filled. Filter narrows the list by name or description. Without a region, hovering a row’s RTT shows the round trip it means to a CDN edge (≈ 93 ms to a CDN edge), the route’s plus its own delay, and hovering the loss of a bursty network says how long its runs are.

Loss is per direction. “In bursts” means lost packets come in runs, as on a weak cell or a busy hotspot, rather than one at a time. The numbers are rough medians from public mobile network reports, rounded to be easy to remember, not measurements of a specific operator. There are no custom values: pick the closest network.

Amber notes under a network say where the route spoils it:

  • The VPN route’s N ms is more than this network’s own N ms: the route is already slower than the network would be (5G behind a far VPN location), so the delay ends up larger than named;
  • Capped at about N Mbit/s by the VPN: the network is faster than the route’s bandwidth.

The network can still be picked: loss, jitter and slower links are unaffected.

Scenarios

Section titled “Scenarios”

Scenarios loop through networks, holding each for a random time within the range given. The Scenarios tab shows one card per scenario with its loop length (20–60 s ↻) and a strip of its steps, each named and roughly as wide as its average hold, with its hold range under it: networks in grey, outages light red and hatched, and the step running now outlined in blue. The running scenario has a Running badge.

Picking a card shows its steps on the right: the link of each, its RTT, download and upload, and how long it is held. While that scenario runs, its current step is highlighted, with a bar and how long it has been held (7 s of 10–20 s, then Outage). Start scenario applies it; Restart scenario on the running one starts it again from the first step.

Scenario Steps

Region

Section titled “Region”

Region stands in for the app’s users in a country. Pick one of Germany, the United States, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Bangladesh, India, Pakistan, Indonesia, the Philippines, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt or Brazil (or No region) in the list on the Region tab; each row shows the region’s typical mobile link, its CDN edge and the edge round trip. A region the route is slower to is marked VPN slower: with CDN, servers or bandwidth. Then pick where your servers run under Servers in on the right: Frankfurt (the default), N. Virginia, N. California, São Paulo, Mumbai, Singapore or Tokyo. Each server location shows the region’s round trip to it, and the route’s in amber when the route is slower (160 ms · VPN 210 ms).

A region splits the device’s traffic into two paths. The Paths table shows them:

Column What it is
Path CDN edge and DNS (the address ranges of Cloudflare, Fastly and Google, served from the edge the region’s users reach, and every DNS query), or Servers in … (your backends and everything not on a CDN)
Region the round trip the region’s users have on that path, beyond their access link
Route what the session’s route already takes on it; amber when that is more than the region’s
Added what is added: the region’s round trip less the route’s, never below 0

The access link comes on top of both paths. With No limits picked as the access link, the first network becomes Typical mobile in the region, the region’s median mobile connection, and that link is used.

When the route is already slower than the region on a path, a warning says so: emulation can only add delay, so a VPN location closer to the servers, or none, gets nearer.

Backends behind a CDN

Section titled “Backends behind a CDN”

An API behind Cloudflare has a CDN address, but its requests go through to your servers. List such hosts under Backends behind a CDN, one per line (or separated by commas): a host name, *.example.com for every name below it, or an IPv4 address. Save backends appears when the list changed.

Their traffic then takes the servers’ path. The agent answers the device’s DNS, so it picks up the addresses the device gets when it looks a backend up, and shapes them about a second later; the addresses found so far are listed under the field. Other hosts on the same CDN addresses take the servers’ path too.

The list is kept per app: in your account when the portal’s database is persistent, in this browser otherwise. It is filled in when you pick a region in the app’s next session.

Common tasks

Section titled “Common tasks”

Check the app on a slow network. Pick 3G or Weak 4G and go through the main screens: loaders should show, and nothing should be tapped twice while a request is slow.

Check that the app survives drops. Pick Subway, Elevator or Flapping. Requests made during an outage hang until they time out; the app should retry or say what happened. The WebSockets tab shows each reconnect of a socket as a new row.

Test for users in a country. Pick the country as the region, your servers’ location in Servers in, and list your API under Backends behind a CDN. A VPN location in or near the same country keeps the route short, so the region’s full round trips can be added; the IP address then also matches the country.

Find out why the emulated network is faster than promised. Read the amber notes: the route is already slower than the network, or the network faster than the route’s bandwidth. Pick a closer VPN location.

See how long requests took. The picker only sets the conditions; the timings are elsewhere (see Measuring the effect).

Measuring the effect

Section titled “Measuring the effect”

Holtdroid has no separate load-speed report. Request timings are in these places:

  • live, in the DevTools tab’s Network panel: Chrome’s own timing for each request of the WebView;
  • in a recording, a request’s Timing on the Network tab: queued and stalled, DNS, connect (with TLS), send, Waiting (TTFB), download and the total;
  • in a recording’s Routes tab, the median connect, TLS and waiting times of the WebView’s requests to each name, and how many of them opened a new connection;
  • the Network row’s Device traffic: download and upload of the whole device every two seconds over the last minute.

In recordings

Section titled “In recordings”

A recording keeps the conditions as they changed: the player’s Network card shows the VPN location, Throttling with the network’s name and numbers (or Full speed), the Region with what it added to the CDN and the servers (CDN +5 ms · Frankfurt +40 ms) and the device’s traffic, each as it was at the moment you are at.

Limits

Section titled “Limits”
  • Only emulator sessions with VPN locations have network conditions; phones don’t.
  • Emulation can only add delay and take bandwidth away, never make the session’s route faster.
  • Addresses are IPv4 only; the CDN ranges are Cloudflare’s, Fastly’s and Google’s published lists.
  • There are no custom networks: the networks and scenarios are fixed.
  • The step shown for a running scenario is the agent’s; the portal reads it again when the step’s shortest hold is over, so it can lag a step’s change by a couple of seconds.
